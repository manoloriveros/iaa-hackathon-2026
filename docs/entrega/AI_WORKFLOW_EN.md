# How I worked with AI

I used Claude and Codex throughout both weeks to explore data, propose and implement methods, challenge results and build verification tools. I also used them for explanation, translation, dashboards and audiovisual preparation. I made the final delivery choices and recorded them separately.

I wanted a workflow I could follow and review as well as competitive predictions. I retained proposals with their criteria, corrections and adverse findings. I did not measure a time saving that I can substantiate.

| Stage | How I used AI | Evidence I retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Data and target | Table exploration, cleaning rules, period matching and claim-valuation questions | Originals, code, variable contracts, ledgers and decisions |
| Modelling | Total-cost and per-peril frequency/severity alternatives, blends and market adaptation | Recipes, parameters, sources and favourable or adverse results |
| Verification | Format, integrity, temporal separation, reproduction and sensitivity checks | Dated receipts, adverse cases, incidents and closure criteria |
| Communication | Method explanations, translation, charts, dashboards and video preparation | Editable sources linked to technical records |

## How I challenged the outputs

AI produced errors as well as useful work. I therefore did not treat persuasive wording or an isolated number as verification. The project compared code with original inputs, checked identifiers and periods, ran adverse cases and used separate verification tools where available. Receipts identify the tested version and scope.

Documented corrections include retrospective reconstruction of audit fields, annual-rate versus period-premium differences, and computational separation when reusing source components. I retain the corrections and their limits in the [document archive](../archivo/README.md) and [project record](../expediente/README.md).

I used different assistants and sessions to challenge proposals. That collaboration remains AI-assisted technical review, not independent professional validation. Reviews retain the actual assistant's attribution; narrating my project in the first person does not change who performed a check.
