In the market with four years of history, I chose an ensemble by cause of loss. One component predicts total claims cost using decision trees and a Tweedie distribution, accommodating zeros and positive costs. Another estimates how often claims occur and how much they cost.

I combined them by line of business because no single model dominated: fire and water damage involve different mechanisms. This captures interactions between variables while explaining risk through frequency and severity. The ensemble's D squared score was zero point one three eight, against zero point zero seven one for a constant rate by line. The calibrated submission scored zero point one three eight six four in the private evaluation, placing fourth among fifty-four teams. During week one, our submissions also topped the public leaderboard.
